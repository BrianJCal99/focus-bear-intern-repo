# Agile Workflows & Kanban

## Research & Learn

A Kanban board is a visual tool used to manage work as it moves through different stages of a process. It helps teams see what tasks are in progress, what is coming next, and what has been completed. By visualizing work, it becomes easier to identify bottlenecks and maintain a steady workflow.

Each column on a Kanban board represents a stage in the workflow. Common columns include:
- **Backlog / Not Started**: Tasks that are planned but not yet worked on  
- **In Progress**: Tasks currently being developed  
- **Blocked**: Tasks that cannot proceed due to issues or dependencies  
- **Ready for Review**: Completed work waiting for feedback or QA  
- **Done / Approved**: Fully completed and accepted tasks  

Tasks move from left to right across the board as work progresses. Typically, developers are responsible for updating the status of their own tasks, while QA or reviewers may move tasks into final stages like “Done” after validation.

Limiting Work In Progress (WIP) means restricting how many tasks can be actively worked on at the same time. This helps reduce context switching, improves focus, and ensures tasks are completed before new ones are started.

## Reflection

Kanban helps manage priorities by making all tasks visible and clearly showing what should be worked on next. Instead of juggling multiple tasks at once, it encourages focusing on a smaller number of high-priority items. This reduces overload and prevents work from piling up in unfinished states.

In my workflow as a front-end and mobile developer, Kanban helps me stay aligned with the team by clearly showing what I’m working on and what’s ready for review. It also makes collaboration with QA and UI/UX smoother since everyone can see the current status of tasks.

I can improve my workflow by being more consistent in updating task statuses in real time. For example, moving tasks immediately when they become blocked or ready for review would give the team better visibility and reduce delays. I can also break down larger tasks into smaller, more manageable items so they move through the board more efficiently.
