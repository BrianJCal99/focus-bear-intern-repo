## Reflection

### When do you prefer using AI vs. searching Google?

Based on the framework I created, I tend to use AI tools first when the problem is not sensitive and I need a quick starting point. AI is especially useful for drafting solutions, explaining concepts, or helping me understand errors when I’m unsure what the issue even is. It fits well in situations where the complexity is moderate and I want to move quickly without getting stuck early.

However, if the problem is simple or factual, I prefer using Google. Searching is more reliable for things like documentation, syntax, or known issues because it leads to official sources and real-world discussions. I also use Google to validate anything AI gives me, especially if the solution will be used in a real project. So in my workflow, AI helps me move fast, while Google helps me confirm accuracy.

### How do you decide when to ask a colleague instead?

The biggest factor for me is whether the problem involves sensitive or project-specific information. If it includes proprietary data or requires deeper understanding of the codebase, I would go straight to a colleague or subject matter expert instead of using AI. This is something I made a clear priority in my flowchart.

I also consider complexity and urgency. If the issue is high-level or strategic, or if I’m working under a tight deadline, I’m more likely to involve a colleague earlier. For example, if something is urgent, I might start with AI for a quick direction but still confirm with a teammate straight away. If I’ve already spent some time trying to solve it and I’m not making progress, that’s usually my signal to stop working alone and ask for help.

### What challenges do developers face when troubleshooting alone?

One challenge is not recognising when to switch approaches. Without a clear strategy, it’s easy to waste time using the wrong tool for the situation, like relying too much on AI for something that actually needs human context, or over-searching simple problems.

Another issue is dealing with complexity without guidance. If a problem is high-level or tied to a specific system, it can be really difficult to make progress alone because you’re missing context. This often leads to trial-and-error instead of structured problem-solving.

Urgency also makes things harder. When there’s time pressure, working alone can increase stress and lead to rushed or incorrect solutions. My framework helped me realise that in urgent cases, it’s better to combine tools (like AI + colleagues) rather than relying on just one.

Overall, troubleshooting alone can slow down learning and productivity if you don’t know when to switch between AI, search, and collaboration. Having a decision-making process makes it more efficient and less frustrating.
