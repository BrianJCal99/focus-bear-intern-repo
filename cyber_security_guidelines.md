## Cyber Security Reflection

### What are common cyber security threats in a remote work environment?

From what I’ve seen, most threats come down to human error rather than highly technical attacks. Phishing emails are probably the biggest one—they often look convincing and try to get me to log into fake websites or share information. Another issue is reusing passwords across multiple accounts, which makes it easier for one breach to affect everything. Using public Wi-Fi and not keeping software updated are also risks that can expose my device without me realising.

### What are best practices for keeping your devices and accounts secure?

I think the basics are what matter most. Keeping my system and apps updated, avoiding suspicious downloads, and being careful about what networks I connect to all help reduce risk. I’ve also realised that setting up things like auto-lock and using built-in security features makes it easier to stay secure without having to think about it constantly.

### Why is it important to lock your computer when away from your desk?

Locking my computer feels like a small thing, but it actually prevents anyone else from accessing my work if I step away. Even if it’s just for a few minutes, it removes the risk of someone seeing or interfering with sensitive information, especially in shared environments.

### How should you handle phishing attempts and suspicious links?

The biggest thing for me is slowing down and not reacting immediately. If something feels urgent or slightly off, I try to double-check it first. I look at the sender’s email carefully and avoid clicking links unless I’m confident they’re legitimate. If I’m unsure, it’s safer to ignore or report it rather than take the risk.

### What makes a strong password, and why should you use a password manager?

I’ve realised that a strong password isn’t just about being complex, but also being unique for every account. In the past, I reused passwords more than I should have. A password manager helps fix that by generating and storing strong passwords, so I don’t have to rely on memory or reuse the same ones.

### Why is two-factor authentication (2FA) important, and when should you enable it?

2FA adds an extra layer of security that makes a big difference. Even if someone gets access to my password, they still can’t log in without the second verification step. I think it’s something that should be enabled on all important accounts, especially anything related to work or personal data.

### What security measures do you currently follow, and where can you improve?

Right now, I keep my devices updated and try to be careful with links, which gives me a decent starting point. However, I know I need to improve by making sure all my passwords are unique and enabling 2FA consistently across all accounts.

### How can you make secure behaviour a habit rather than an afterthought?

For me, it’s about reducing the effort required. Using a password manager, enabling auto-lock, and relying on default security settings means I don’t have to think about it every time. Over time, these actions become part of my normal workflow instead of something I have to remember.

### What steps will you take to ensure your passwords and accounts are secure?

I plan to properly set up and use a password manager, update any weak or reused passwords, and enable 2FA wherever possible. These steps are straightforward but make a big difference overall.

### What would you do if you suspected a security breach or suspicious activity on your account?

If something seems off, I would act quickly by changing my passwords and logging out of all sessions. After that, I’d report the issue and check my device for any potential problems. The priority would be to secure the account as soon as possible and prevent further access.

### Task Completion

I’ve reviewed my current setup and identified areas to improve. I’ve either enabled or plan to enable 2FA on important accounts and am moving towards properly using a password manager. I’ve also set my devices to lock automatically after a short period.

**New cyber security habit:**
I will take a moment to verify emails and links before clicking on anything, especially if it involves logging in or sharing sensitive information.
